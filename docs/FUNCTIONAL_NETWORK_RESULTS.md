**Widely distributed KOs form functional co-abundance modules**

Using gene annotations and CLR-transformed KO counts from 191 metagenomes, we identified groups of functions with similar abundance patterns across mangrove sediments. The network contained 1,062 associations among 327 connected KOs. Six major groups, termed modules, included 236 KOs, of which 194 (82.2%) were near-global (Fig. 5a; Supplementary Table S14). M01 and M06 were enriched in information-processing and transport/membrane functions, respectively (2.15- and 2.62-fold), with support under both annotation treatments. Enrichment of carbon/carbohydrate functions in M03 (3.45-fold) depended on the annotation source (Fig. 5b; Supplementary Tables S15–S22).

KOs within the same module generally remained grouped when some network links were removed. To determine whether these functional groups also depended on particular sources of samples, we reconstructed the network after temporarily omitting all samples from each BioProject in turn. The largest change occurred when six metagenomes belonging to one BioProject were omitted: the number of major modules decreased from six to four, and the associations and module composition changed substantially (Fig. 5c; Supplementary Tables S23, S26 and S27). All 191 metagenomes were retained in the primary analysis. Thus, the modules describe groups of microbial functions whose abundances vary together in the sampled sediments, but their organization is sensitive to the samples included. These associations may reflect genes carried by the same microorganisms or similar environmental responses among their carriers; they do not establish direct biochemical interactions or metabolic activity.

Figure 5 | Functional co-abundance modules are dominated by widely distributed KOs. (a) Six major modules inferred from 191 metagenomes and 1,500 candidate KOs. The displayed subnetwork contains 236 KOs and 999 associations; the full network contains 327 connected KOs and 1,062 associations. Colours identify modules; labels give enriched functional categories and total KO counts. Diverse functions denotes no supported category enrichment. Squares, circles and triangles represent absolute-core, near-global and realm-core KOs, respectively. (b) Primary category enrichment supported by both Benjamini–Yekutieli q ≤ 0.05 and maxT family-wise P ≤ 0.05 across 4,999 blocked permutations and 42 module–category comparisons. Point sizes and labels give category KO counts; the dashed line denotes no enrichment. The open point and asterisk mark support sensitive to adding partial pathway annotations. (c) Membership sensitivity with 50-start Leiden re-estimation. Circles show mean within-module co-assignment across 500 edge perturbations; diamonds show the median across 43 BioProject omissions, with horizontal lines spanning the observed range. Edges were retained in perturbations according to their consensus selection frequencies. Higher co-assignment indicates that pairs remain together but does not penalize mergers. These ranges are not confidence intervals.
