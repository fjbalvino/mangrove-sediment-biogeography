**Co-abundance modules organize widely distributed functions**

Using gene annotations and CLR-transformed KO counts from 191 metagenomes, we inferred a consensus co-abundance network with 1,060 associations among 327 KOs. Five major modules contained 238 KOs, of which 197 (82.8%) were near-global. Three modules were enriched in information processing, amino acid and cofactor biosynthesis, and transport/membrane functions (Fig. 6a,b). The information-processing and transport modules showed the highest stability under edge perturbation; membership in the remaining modules varied more across reconstructions (Fig. 6c). Module boundaries were also sensitive to consensus thresholds and community-detection algorithms (Supplementary Results). These co-abundance associations may reflect shared environmental responses, common genomic carriers or pathway membership; they do not establish direct biochemical interactions.

## Supplementary Results

The full network comprised 327 connected KOs and 1,060 consensus associations. The five major modules contained 65, 60, 59, 42 and 12 KOs, respectively; their subnetwork contained 989 associations. Persistence classes among these 238 KOs were absolute core (9), near-global (197) and realm-core (32). Near-global predominance was descriptive: its enrichment in major modules relative to connected nodes was not supported after matching occurrence, abundance, CLR variance, degree and strength (one-sided P = 0.2726; BY q = 1.0). The corresponding test for connected nodes against the 1,500 candidates was also unsupported (P = 0.5700; BY q = 1.0).

Three module–category pairs passed both multiple-testing corrections: information processing in M01 (29 KOs; 2.08-fold enrichment; BY q = 0.00968; maxT P = 0.0002), amino acid/cofactor biosynthesis in M04 (14 KOs; 2.34-fold; q = 0.00968; maxT P = 0.0026), and transport/membrane systems in M05 (11 KOs; 3.97-fold; q = 0.00968; maxT P = 0.0002). No anaerobic redox/energy category passed the joint criterion. Broad categories were assigned using the frozen functional dictionary and the documented text-matching rules; 15 of the 238 major-module KOs had tied category scores, resolved by the existing rule order, and 81 KOs were classified as uncharacterized/other. KEGG MODULE enrichment was not inferred because the supplied mapping covered historical network nodes only.

Mean within-module co-assignment probabilities under 500 edge perturbations were 0.866 (M01), 0.420 (M02), 0.388 (M03), 0.516 (M04) and 0.936 (M05). The median adjusted Rand index against the primary partition was 0.530 (5th–95th percentiles, 0.402–0.666). Across 30 consensus-threshold combinations, adjusted Rand indices ranged from 0.367 to 0.551, and 91.6–100% of primary major-module nodes remained connected. Alternative whole-graph community detection gave adjusted Rand indices of 0.593 for another Louvain partition, 0.441 for Infomap, 0.400 for Walktrap and 0.522 for Leiden. These results support greater stability of the information-processing and transport modules, with appreciable uncertainty in the remaining module boundaries.

Nominal assortativity by persistence class was 0.245, compared with a null mean of −0.00745 across 999 degree-preserving rewires (one-sided P = 0.001; two-sided P = 0.002). The fraction of edges joining two near-global KOs was 0.785, compared with a null mean of 0.757 (one-sided P = 0.001; two-sided P = 0.002). These graph-level results do not establish biochemical interactions or independence from shared environmental or genomic influences.

## Supplementary Methods

Nonzero KO counts were selected using the current 191-profile accession manifest. KOs occurring in at least 10 profiles and with at least 10 total assignments formed the association universe (13,308 KOs). A pseudocount of 1 was added before CLR transformation within this universe. Network eligibility required occurrence in at least 20 profiles and at least 50 total assignments. Of 10,614 eligible KOs, the 5,000 with greatest CLR variance were retained, followed by 1,500 network candidates. Variance ties were resolved by KO identifier. One hundred subsamples without replacement retained at most four profiles from each marine realm × country × locality stratum. The 26 strata yielded 81 profiles per subsample; this cap limits local replication but does not equalize realm sample sizes or remove study effects.

Proportionality was calculated as twice the covariance divided by the sum of variances in CLR space. Each subsample retained the 2,000 strongest absolute associations. Consensus edges required selection frequency ≥ 0.80, complete sign consistency and mean absolute proportionality ≥ 0.95. Weighted Louvain clustering identified communities; modules with at least 10 KOs were interpreted. Enrichment used 4,999 blocked permutations conditional on occurrence, total abundance and CLR variance, with degree and absolute strength included in module tests. Supported categories required both BY q ≤ 0.05 and permutation maxT family-wise P ≤ 0.05. These are conditional post-selection tests on KOs and graph partitions. Network sensitivity used 999 degree-preserving rewires, 30 threshold combinations, four community algorithms and 500 edge perturbations, with communities inferred on the whole candidate graph throughout. The analysis used seed 123, R 4.3.3, data.table 1.17.8 and igraph 2.2.1.
