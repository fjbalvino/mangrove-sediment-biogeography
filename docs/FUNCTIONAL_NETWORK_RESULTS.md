**Co-abundance modules organize widely distributed functions**

Using gene annotations and CLR-transformed KO counts from 191 metagenomes, we inferred a consensus co-abundance network to examine how functions covaried across sampling locations. The network contained 1,060 associations among 327 KOs. Five major modules comprised 238 KOs, of which 197 (82.8%) were near-global. Three modules were enriched in information processing, amino acid and cofactor biosynthesis, and transport/membrane functions (Fig. 6a,b). The information-processing and transport modules showed the most consistent membership under edge perturbation (Fig. 6c). Membership in the other modules was less stable, and alternative community-detection algorithms produced different module boundaries (Supplementary Results). These associations may reflect shared environmental responses, common genomic carriers or pathway membership; they do not establish direct biochemical interactions.

Figure 6 | Functional co-abundance modules are dominated by widely distributed KOs. (a) Five major modules, each containing at least 10 KOs, extracted from the consensus network inferred from 191 metagenomes. The displayed subnetwork contains 238 KOs and 989 associations; the full network contains 327 connected KOs and 1,060 associations. Colours indicate modules, shapes indicate functional persistence classes, and labels give module sizes. (b) Functional categories supported by both Benjamini–Yekutieli q ≤ 0.05 and permutation maxT family-wise P ≤ 0.05 across 4,999 blocked KO permutations. Points show fold enrichment; point size and labels give the number of KOs in each category. The dashed line indicates no enrichment. (c) Mean within-module co-assignment probability across 500 edge perturbations. Each edge was retained with probability equal to its consensus selection frequency, and communities were inferred again on the complete candidate graph. Values describe stability of module membership, not temporal stability of the functions. Complete enrichment tests and sensitivity analyses are provided in Supplementary Results.
