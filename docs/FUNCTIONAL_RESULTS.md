**A widely shared functional backbone remains geographically structured**

Functional analyses used the 191 of 341 taxonomically profiled metagenomes represented in the filtered protein-annotation matrix. Of the 17,482 KEGG orthologues (KOs) detected, only 24 occurred in every metagenome and constituted the absolute core. Among the remaining KOs, those detected in at least 95% of metagenomes were classified as near-global. Realm-core KOs were then defined as those detected in every sampled locality of at least one realm represented by three or more localities, with a mean relative abundance of at least 0.01% within that realm. All other KOs were assigned to the functional periphery.

Near-global and realm-core classes contained 1,104 and 1,415 KOs, respectively, and together accounted for 82.0% of KO annotation counts. Including the absolute core, these shared classes represented 14.5% of KO richness but 86.2% of annotation counts. Most functional richness belonged to the periphery: 14,939 KOs contributed only 13.8% of counts (Fig. 4a; Supplementary Table S11). These counts quantify predicted proteins assigned to KO annotations.

Principal component analysis of the functional profiles showed differentiation between the Tropical Atlantic (TA) and Central Indo-Pacific (CIP; Fig. 4b). A PERMANOVA comparing one mean profile per sampling site supported this contrast (R² = 0.275, Holm-adjusted P < 0.001), without evidence of unequal dispersion. Realm-core and near-global KOs contributed 64.3% and 27.4%, respectively, of the summed absolute loadings of the 100 KOs most strongly associated with PC1. However, PC1 closely tracked the total number of KO assignments per profile. The TA–CIP contrast persisted after accounting for this total, but its unique contribution fell to 2.5% of variation (Supplementary Fig. S4; Supplementary Table S12). Because individual studies were confined to single realms, these patterns could not be separated fully from study differences.

Read-based taxonomic and assembly-based functional profiles were compared using the same 191 metagenomes. Their compositional dissimilarities were positively correlated (Mantel r = 0.678, P = 0.0001; Fig. 4c), and the association persisted when each site contributed a single mean profile. Among the 184 metagenomes from 57 sites with complete coordinates, functional similarity declined more slowly with geographic distance than taxonomic similarity (functional slope, −0.048; taxonomic slope, −0.059; Fig. 4d). This difference was supported by site-level comparisons and remained consistent across 1,000 selections of one metagenome per site (Supplementary Table S13). These results are consistent with partial retention of encoded functional potential during taxonomic turnover.
