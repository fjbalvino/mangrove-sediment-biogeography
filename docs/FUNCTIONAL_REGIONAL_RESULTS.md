# Regional differentiation varies across functional prevalence classes

Of the 17,482 KOs detected in 191 metagenomes, 1,128 were classified as Prevalent (detected in ≥95% of metagenomes), 9,886 as Shell (15% to <95%) and 6,468 as Cloud (<15%; Fig. 5a). Shell KOs did not consistently show the strongest regional differentiation. In balanced comparisons among three sufficiently replicated marine realms, median Aitchison PERMANOVA R² was 0.157 for Prevalent, 0.140 for Shell and 0.118 for Cloud KOs. This ordering also occurred in the alternative sampling design. Within Shell, realm-core KOs showed greater median differentiation than peripheral KOs (R² = 0.168 versus 0.143; Fig. 5b). Sampling sensitivities and dispersion diagnostics are reported in the Supplementary Results.

Selected Shell functions showed distinct regional patterns (Fig. 5c). The thiocyanate-degradation score was highest in the Tropical Atlantic and had the largest median regional R² among the six selected process groups (0.270; 5th–95th percentiles across balanced selections, 0.115–0.514). Scores for anaerobic sulfur respiration, methanogenic/Hdr electron transfer and anaerobic nitrate and sulfite respiration were highest in the Central Indo-Pacific. However, the relative strength of these patterns depended on total KO annotation counts: conditioning on this total reduced the thiocyanate score’s median unique realm R² to 0.068. These process scores describe variation in selected annotations, not their contribution to total functional variation or metabolic activity.

Shell composition also varied within realms (Fig. 5d). Among Tropical Atlantic localities, RMS Aitchison distance from the equally weighted global locality centroid ranged from 0.298 in Francisco do Conde to 0.934 in Guadeloupe. The map displays 23 georeferenced localities, while all 26 localities contribute to the centroid. Together, these analyses describe regional and local variation in encoded functional potential, without supporting a general Shell advantage or dominance by nitrogen, sulfur and methanogenesis pathways. Because studies were confined to individual realms, geographic patterns remain inseparable from study differences.

Figure 5 | Regional variation in functional classes and selected Shell processes.

(a) Prevalent, Shell and Cloud KOs cross-classified against absolute-core, near-global, realm-core and peripheral persistence classes across 17,482 KOs from 191 metagenomes. Cells show KO counts and percentages of all detected functional features; shading follows log₁₀(count + 1). (b) Regional differentiation among eligible persistence–prevalence intersections. Points and lines show median Aitchison PERMANOVA R² and 5th–95th percentiles across 499 balanced selections from Central Indo-Pacific, Tropical Atlantic and Western Indo-Pacific. Each selection included six localities per realm and one metagenome per locality; tests used 499 permutations. (c) Regional patterns of six selected functional groups comprising 33 Shell KOs; n denotes the number of KOs in each group. Groups were retained from the original functional catalogue, with membership restricted to current Shell KOs. Scores average the corresponding Shell centred log-ratio (CLR) coordinates and are standardized across 191 metagenomes. Heatmap values show balanced regional mean scores; adjacent points and lines show median regional R² and 5th–95th percentiles across the selections in b. R² measures realm-associated variation in each score. (d) Geographic variation in Shell composition among localities. Colours show root-mean-square Aitchison distance between each locality’s mean Shell CLR profile and the equally weighted centroid of all 26 localities; higher values indicate greater compositional deviation. The map displays 23 georeferenced localities representing 184 metagenomes; three localities without coordinates remain in the centroid. Point area indicates metagenome count, capped at 20; insets enlarge Brazil and South China. This panel describes variation within Shell and does not compare its explanatory strength with other prevalence classes. CLR transformations use a pseudocount of one. Percentiles describe sampling sensitivity, not population confidence intervals.
