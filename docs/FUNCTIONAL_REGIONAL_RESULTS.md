# Regional differentiation varies across functional prevalence classes

Of the 17,482 KOs detected in 191 metagenomes, 1,128 were classified as Prevalent (detected in ≥95% of metagenomes), 9,886 as Shell (15% to <95%) and 6,468 as Cloud (<15%; Fig. 6a). Shell KOs did not consistently show the strongest regional differentiation. In balanced comparisons among three sufficiently replicated marine realms, median Aitchison PERMANOVA R² was 0.157 for Prevalent, 0.140 for Shell and 0.118 for Cloud KOs. This ordering also occurred in the alternative sampling design. Within Shell, realm-core KOs showed greater median differentiation than peripheral KOs (R² = 0.168 versus 0.143; Fig. 6b). Sampling sensitivities and dispersion diagnostics are reported in the Supplementary Results.

Selected Shell annotation sets also differed among regions (Fig. 6c; Supplementary Tables S31 and S32). Sulfur redox, Hdr and hydrogenase, and nitrate/nitrite and sulfite redox components had their highest mean scores in the Central Indo-Pacific. The thiocyanate-hydrolase subunit score was highest in the Tropical Atlantic and had the largest median regional R² (0.270; 5th–95th percentiles, 0.115–0.514), but its conditional unique R² fell to 0.068 after accounting for total annotation counts. All three subunits co-occurred in 32 profiles, falling to seven when two records per subunit were required. These contrasts describe selected annotations and do not establish complete pathways, activity or net reaction direction.

Shell composition also varied within realms (Fig. 6d; Supplementary Fig. S7). Among Tropical Atlantic localities, RMS Aitchison distance from the equally weighted global locality centroid ranged from 0.298 in Francisco do Conde to 0.934 in Guadeloupe. Across all 26 mapped localities, Hainan had the largest distance (1.113). These patterns show local variation within the shared regional setting, without supporting a general Shell advantage. Because studies were confined to individual realms, geographic patterns remain inseparable from study differences.

Figure 6 | Regional variation in functional classes and selected Shell annotation sets.

(a) Prevalent, Shell and Cloud KOs cross-classified against absolute-core, near-global, realm-core and peripheral persistence classes across 17,482 KOs from 191 metagenomes. Cells show KO counts and percentages of all detected functional features; shading follows log₁₀(count + 1). (b) Regional differentiation among eligible persistence–prevalence intersections. Points and lines show median Aitchison PERMANOVA R² and 5th–95th percentiles across 499 balanced selections from Central Indo-Pacific, Tropical Atlantic and Western Indo-Pacific. Each selection included six localities per realm and one metagenome per locality; tests used 499 permutations. (c) Regional patterns of six selected functional groups comprising 33 Shell KOs; n denotes the number of KOs in each group. KO membership was retained from the original catalogue and restricted to current Shell KOs; labels describe annotated components rather than complete pathways. Scores average the corresponding Shell centred log-ratio (CLR) coordinates and are standardized across 191 metagenomes. Heatmap values show balanced regional mean scores; adjacent points and lines show median regional R² and 5th–95th percentiles across the selections in b. R² measures realm-associated variation in each score. (d) Geographic variation in Shell composition among localities. Colours show root-mean-square Aitchison distance between each locality’s mean Shell CLR profile and the equally weighted centroid of all 26 localities; higher values indicate greater compositional deviation. The map displays all 26 localities representing 191 metagenomes. Locations summarize the updated coordinates and retain any locality-level uncertainty. Point area indicates metagenome count, capped at 20; insets enlarge Brazil and South China. This panel describes variation within Shell and does not compare its explanatory strength with other prevalence classes. CLR transformations use a pseudocount of one. Percentiles describe sampling sensitivity, not population confidence intervals.
