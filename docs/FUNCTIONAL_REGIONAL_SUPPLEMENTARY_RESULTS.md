# Supplementary results: functional prevalence and regional differentiation

## Cohort and persistence reconciliation

The analysis retained all 191 accessions in the canonical functional manifest, including TX1_3 and all 23 Guadeloupe profiles. The source matrix contained 194 profiles; the three documented air controls (SRR31095011, SRR31095534 and SRR31095535) were excluded. Removing 26 KOs with zero counts in the retained cohort left 17,482 KOs. All recomputed persistence labels agreed with the current functional atlas. The periphery comprised 14,939 KOs (85.45% of richness) and 13.79% of KO annotation assignments. The remaining KOs comprised 24 absolute-core, 1,104 near-global and 1,415 realm-core functions. All realm-core KOs fell within Shell; the peripheral fraction contained 8,471 Shell and 6,468 Cloud KOs.

The corrected cohort contained 82 Central Indo-Pacific profiles from 10 named localities, 76 Tropical Atlantic profiles from seven localities, 30 Western Indo-Pacific profiles from eight localities and three Tropical Eastern Pacific profiles from one locality. All 191 profiles contributed to prevalence and persistence classification. The first three realms supplied 188 eligible profiles for balanced regional comparisons. Tropical Eastern Pacific could not contribute to a replicated locality-level comparison. Named localities are the `core_locality` units used in persistence classification; they are distinct from coordinate-defined sampling sites used in other functional analyses.

## Sampling and annotation-total sensitivity

Shell exceeded both Prevalent and Cloud in 205 of 499 primary selections (41.1%) and 188 of 499 alternative selections (37.7%). Median paired contrasts were −0.009 (5th–95th percentiles, −0.072 to 0.061) and −0.023 (−0.096 to 0.112), respectively. These distributions did not support a consistent Shell advantage. Conditioning on log₁₀ total KO annotation counts yielded a primary median contrast of −0.058 (−0.115 to −0.036), with no positive contrasts among 499 selections. In the alternative design, the adjusted contrast was −0.091 (−0.167 to −0.042); two of 499 contrasts were positive. Here, adjusted R² denotes the unique realm term divided by total sum of squares, not a degrees-of-freedom-corrected R².

Equalizing feature counts to 1,128 KOs per class, requiring occurrence in at least ten profiles and changing prevalence boundaries to 90%/10% or 97.5%/20% all produced negative median unadjusted Shell contrasts. Their sampling intervals crossed zero. Six country deletions and 42 study/BioProject deletions remained compatible with the fixed three-realm × six-locality design. Country-deletion medians ranged from −0.028 to 0.040; study/BioProject-deletion medians ranged from −0.016 to 0.040. Excluding Brazil, China or India, or study/BioProject PRJNA954358, left insufficient locality replication and was explicitly recorded as non-estimable. These omissions prevent a claim of robustness to every country or study deletion.

## Dispersion and study structure

Shell PERMDISP reached nominal P ≤ 0.05 in three of 499 primary selections (0.6%) and nine of 499 alternative selections (1.8%). In the primary design, corresponding frequencies were 11/499 (2.2%) for Prevalent and 57/499 (11.4%) for Cloud. Tests used spatial medians with a small-sample bias adjustment. The low Shell frequency indicates infrequent detection of dispersion differences under these selections; it does not demonstrate equal dispersion or validate a causal geographic interpretation. All 44 study identifiers were nested within one realm, including 43 identifiers in the three comparison realms. Consequently, a realm effect conditioned on study could not be identified separately. Individual archive identifiers, especially the sample-level Guadeloupe BioProjects, should not be interpreted as independent published studies.

## Selected Shell process scores

The six process groups were retained from the original Figure 5 catalog without selecting functions on the basis of the new effect sizes. All 34 catalog KOs were present in the current matrix. K00441 changed from Shell in the historical cohort (182/194 profiles, 93.81%) to Prevalent in the current cohort (182/191, 95.29%) and was excluded from the Shell-only score. The six groups therefore contained eight KOs for sulfur redox components; six for Hdr and hydrogenase components; six for nitrate/nitrite and sulfite redox components; three for thiocyanate-hydrolase subunits; six for aromatic-compound transformation components; and four for EnvZ-OmpR and KdpD-KdpE regulators. This fixed catalog is a selected annotation set, not an exhaustive pathway inventory.

Counts for all 9,886 Shell KOs were transformed to CLR with a pseudocount of one. Each process score was the equally weighted mean of its selected KO coordinates in this full Shell subcomposition. Each score was then standardized across all 191 profiles using the sample standard deviation. It was not recentered within the selected process, which would force its mean to zero. Regional means and effect sizes used the exact 499 profile selections from each existing balanced design. For each score, regional R² was between-realm sum of squares divided by total score sum of squares. A complementary model measured unique realm sum of squares conditional on log₁₀ total KO annotation counts, again using total score sum of squares as denominator. No process-level significance or pathway-enrichment test was performed.

In the primary design, thiocyanate-hydrolase subunits had the largest median unadjusted score R² (0.270), followed by EnvZ-OmpR and KdpD-KdpE regulators (0.188), nitrate/nitrite and sulfite redox components (0.150), Hdr and hydrogenase components (0.125), sulfur redox components (0.081), and aromatic-compound transformation components (0.079). Balanced mean thiocyanate scores were 0.701 in Tropical Atlantic, −0.318 in Central Indo-Pacific and −0.319 in Western Indo-Pacific. Environmental sensing and osmoregulation was highest in Western Indo-Pacific (0.456). All other selected scores were highest in Central Indo-Pacific. Standardized scores refer to selected annotations relative to the Shell geometric mean; they are not absolute abundances or rates.

After conditioning on total annotation counts, median unique realm R² was highest for Hdr and hydrogenase components (0.145), followed by sulfur redox components (0.095), nitrate/nitrite and sulfite redox components (0.073), thiocyanate-hydrolase subunits (0.068), aromatic-compound transformation components (0.028), and EnvZ-OmpR and KdpD-KdpE regulators (0.025). Conditional and unadjusted R² need not decrease for every score because the count covariate can suppress or share regional associations. The alternative design retained thiocyanate-hydrolase subunits and environmental sensing as the two highest unadjusted scores (0.305 and 0.249). Neither comparison measures how much of total Shell compositional variation is jointly attributable to these processes.

The six sets are selected annotation groups, not pathway-completeness rules. The sulfur set combines Phs/Psr, Ttr and Apr components; Apr homologues occur in both sulfur-reducing and sulfur-oxidizing organisms (Meyer and Kuever, 2007). The Hdr/hydrogenase set does not include the terminal methane-forming Mcr complex, and Hdr-like systems also participate in bacterial sulfur oxidation (Koch and Dahl, 2018). K00370 and K00371 include both nitrate-reductase and nitrite-oxidoreductase assignments, so their KO identifiers alone do not resolve reaction direction (KEGG K00370 and K00371). The nitrate/nitrite–sulfite set also combines distinct enzyme systems and omits AsrC; it is not a single respiratory chain. The aromatic set combines different substrates and partial systems. EnvZ-OmpR and KdpD-KdpE describe regulatory pairs rather than demonstrated osmotic adaptation.

ScnA, ScnB and ScnC were evaluated separately as the three structural subunits of thiocyanate hydrolase (Katayama et al., 1998). Their KOs were individually detected in 41, 63 and 105 of 191 profiles, with 55, 91 and 247 total annotation records, respectively. All three were co-detected in 32 profiles at one record per subunit (Central Indo-Pacific, 14/82; Tropical Atlantic, 11/76; Western Indo-Pacific, 7/30; Tropical Eastern Pacific, 0/3), and in seven profiles at two records (4/82, 3/76, 0/30 and 0/3, respectively). These community-level checks do not establish a complete enzyme in one organism. The relative annotation-score contrast and its sensitivity to total annotation recovery should therefore remain exploratory.

## Locality Shell turnover

The map retained all 191 profiles and all 9,886 Shell KOs. For each profile, counts were transformed to CLR with a pseudocount of one. Profile CLR coordinates were averaged within each named realm–country–locality unit, and the resulting 26 locality centroids received equal weight in the global centroid. Locality turnover was the square root of the mean squared difference across Shell CLR coordinates from that global centroid, equivalent to Aitchison distance divided by the square root of the number of Shell KOs. Unlike a site-level analysis, this estimator gives each named locality one contribution regardless of its number of profiles or distinct coordinate-defined sites.

All 26 named localities now have coordinates for every included profile, representing 191 metagenomes. The updated metadata provide the seven previously unavailable coordinate pairs for Guanxi (three profiles), Hainan (three) and Rabigh Lagoon (one). Map positions use the component-wise medians of complete coordinate pairs within each locality. Recovered locality approximations remain approximate; this update does not improve their original spatial precision. Sample identities, realm and locality assignments, functional scores, centroid distances and balanced selections were unchanged. The three Tropical Eastern Pacific profiles from Buenaventura contribute to the descriptive map and centroid, although that realm is ineligible for balanced regional comparisons.

Turnover ranged from 0.298 in Francisco do Conde to 1.113 in Hainan across all 26 localities. Guanxi and Rabigh Lagoon had values of 1.042 and 0.759, respectively. Locality differences were not uniform within realms: for example, both Francisco do Conde and Guadeloupe were in Tropical Atlantic. Distances are descriptive measures of compositional departure from this sampled reference centroid; they do not estimate geographic distance-decay, endemicity or a mechanism of turnover.

Uncertainty was summarized by 500 bootstrap resamples of profiles with replacement within each locality, retaining its original profile count and recalculating the equally weighted global centroid each time. The map uses the direct all-profile estimate; 5th–95th bootstrap percentiles are reported separately. These percentiles describe within-locality sampling variation, not uncertainty from selecting localities, studies or environmental conditions. A singleton locality has no within-locality resampling variation; its distance can nevertheless vary because the global centroid changes. Bootstrap medians need not equal the direct estimator and the percentile interval can fail to contain it.
